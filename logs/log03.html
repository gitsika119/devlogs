I'm 21, it's 2026, and for as long as I can remember, "Google it," "search it up," or "my dad told me" had been the default answers to "I don't know." You searched it up, skipped the ads, and went straight to the first Reddit result that came up.

But over the last four years, that "Google it" silently became "Chat it", and EVERYONE realizes it.

This is just my take on the topic. I'm not an etymology expert, a psychologist, or a subject matter expert on anything. I'm just someone who likes to yap and muse and dilly-dally and frolic and... you get the gist of it.

The Path of Least Resistance

I feel like it's just a convenience thing. Like water, people naturally take the path of least resistance, and if something is annoying, people will (consciously or unconsciously) avoid it. Think about scrolling past the AI-generated summary on a search page just to find a Reddit thread matching your particular issue, when the summary already very likely gathered its information from that exact same thread.

Why go through the friction of hunting through links when the tool just gives it to you straight?

The Erosion of the Bedrock

But just like water erodes and weathers the bedrock while flowing, there's a catch. I feel like people are creating enough AI slop out there now for AI to start feeding on its own slop, causing it to hallucinate. Which is hilarious, but also a reality check. It makes you realize that digging a bit deeper just to manually confirm Chat's answers might actually be a sound idea.

My final thoughts on this? I don't know, actually. I'm human too, and like most of us, that path of least resistance is quite appealing. We are going to keep chatting it, we just have to make sure we don't forget how to verify it.
